What runs on the board for the evaluation, as it is.

On the board

Qualcomm QIDK (Snapdragon 8 Gen 3, SM8650), Hexagon V75 NPU, Android 14. Everything below runs on the board alone, offline. Two models share the NPU through one daemon, which swaps between them.

How an incident flows today
  1. Sensor readings arrive every 5 min (the demo replays real campus data, 1 reading every 2 s). The rules watch every sensor.
  2. Critical fast path: 2 readings in a row past a critical limit open the incident at once, with a rules-only report.
  3. Event gate: 8 abnormal readings start the AI: one domain agent per flagged sensor, all decoded together on the NPU.
  4. Preliminary report (rules plus the agents' findings) on screen within seconds.
  5. Reasoner report (Qwen3.5-4B) replaces it: title and severity first, then the full report.
  6. Re-checks at 1.5 h and 3 h re-read the sensors with a longer window and can raise or lower the severity. The operator approves every action.

Known limits
  • Sensor time dominates. A non-critical event reaches the AI after 8 readings, 40 to 55 min of real sensor time. Only sensors with a critical limit have the fast path; power, tank level and flow have no fixed limits.
  • Heat. Back-to-back heavy incidents push the board to 90 to 94 °C and the thermal guard trips. The board needs a heatsink or fan in the field.
  • The NPU is not bit-for-bit repeatable. The same prompt can give a different answer near a decision boundary: severity agrees 97% of the time across repeats (100% with the guard).
  • Known model weaknesses. Recurring small spikes, pump cycling, and erratic or exponential shapes at 3 h are still often misnamed. Hourly sensors (such as TDS) have too few points and stay rules-only.
  • One NPU, two models. Swapping between the agents' model and the reasoner costs about 3 to 4 s per incident; bursts queue.
  • Data. All models were trained and tested on one campus's sensors (test months held out, about 15% of sensors held out completely).
What's next
  • After the evaluation: the pruned output layer A/B (about −12% reasoner time; held back because the board rebooted once with it) and the GPU benchmark.
  • Reasoner on the GPU, agents on the NPU: no model swap, and the next incident's agents can run while a report is written. Needs a memory coexistence test first.
  • Younger events: use the gate study to switch on the early, provisional AI report for the shapes the agents read reliably at 3 to 7 readings.
  • Storage and fog: the last 1,000 incidents on the board plus an all-time index; derived data (never raw readings) to a free cloud database, with the board as the only writer.
