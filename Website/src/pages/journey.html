Journey · 6 to 29 October 2026

From one slow model call to a two-model edge system.

Eleven steps, oldest first. Each says what we did, why, and what we measured on the QIDK. Steps we tried and dropped are listed too, because they explain why things are the way they are.

kept, runs today kept, later replaced measured and dropped
  1. 1 · 6 to 14 Oct · Start

    Fine-tuned Llama 3.2 3B on the NPU through Qualcomm Genie

    What
    One small model per building domain (air, water, power, occupancy, weather), fine-tuned with QLoRA on 6,480 worked examples from real IIIT-H sensor data, quantised to 4 bits and run on the Hexagon NPU with Qualcomm's Genie runtime. Three modes: rules only, one AI call, or one agent per flagged sensor plus a synthesiser.
    Why
    Faults should be explained on the device, offline, with evidence, not only flagged by thresholds.
    Result
    Pattern accuracy 94% for the fine-tuned model against 68% for the rules and 20% for the base model (laptop, 150 held-out windows); about 85% on the NPU after GPTQ quantisation (54 windows). One NPU call cost about 1.2 s plus 52 ms per output token, so a 12-agent incident took 46 to 48 s.
  2. 2 · 16 Oct · Runtime

    htpd: our own batched NPU daemon on llama.cpp's Hexagon backend

    What
    One long-lived process keeps the model on the NPU and decodes all agents of an incident together, so each pass over the weights serves every agent. Added per-stream attention on the NPU, pipelined matrix units, verified drafting and a cache for the fixed part of each prompt.
    Why
    Genie silently ignored batching. The Adreno GPU was slower (about 8.4 s per agent, no batching gain) and a second model on it rebooted the board.
    Result
    12 agents plus synthesiser: 46 to 48 s → 20.7 s through the app. Accuracy on 54 device windows did not drop: pattern 85.2% → 90.7%.
  3. 3 · 16 Oct · Windows

    Window study, refined re-analysis and 12-hour checks

    What
    Measured 2, 4, 8 and 12 h windows (2,416 NPU calls) and where an event sits inside the window (1,248 calls). Added automatic re-analysis 1.5 h and 3 h after the alert, and CPU checks for level shifts and drifts over 12 h.
    Why
    The AI is called while an event is young, but it had been trained on older events.
    Result
    4 h was the best single window. Pattern accuracy was 42% at 40 min after onset and 88% at 3 h, which is why the report is refined later. The 12 h checks caught old steps 73% → 93% and drifts 83% → 95% with no new false alarms.
  4. 4 · 16 to 21 Oct · Agents v2

    Domain agents retrained on what v1 got wrong

    What
    35,946 training rows for six roles (a new HVAC agent), windows of 2 to 12 h, young and old events, sensor faults, and normal routines that look like alarms. About 50 h on the laptop GPU. In the app, the re-analysis window now depends on the event's shape and age.
    Why
    v1 misread young events and called about 31% of normal routines alarms.
    Result
    At 40 min: 42% → 73.8%. False alarms 31% → 18%. With the shape-based window, 86.3% at 3 h on a fresh seed (182 events).
  5. 5 · 21 to 25 Oct · Hardening

    Rules after the model, a weight blend, and many rejected top-ups

    What
    Deterministic checks applied after each agent answer (limits, glitches, chatter, allowed causes, leak and HVAC rules). The agents' model became mixA50, a 50/50 weight blend of v2 and a top-up. A real-world data layer stopped the app inventing readings for silent sensors. Approval workflow for operators.
    Why
    Several further training runs (3d, 3e, v2.1) looked better on the laptop but were no better, or worse, on the NPU. We now screen every candidate on a host copy of the board model, which matches the board on 207 of 208 rows.
    Result
    Status accuracy 81.5% → 86.4% on 1,148 test rows and 60.4% → 85.2% on 318 real-world rows, from the post-rules alone.
  6. 6 · 23 to 27 Oct · Reasoner

    A cross-domain reasoner (Qwen3.5-4B) on the same NPU

    What
    Qwen3.5-4B, fine-tuned with QLoRA (rank 32, about 21 h on the laptop), replaces the synthesiser. It reads the agents' findings and writes the title, severity, root cause, evidence and checklist. htpd swaps between the agents' model and the reasoner.
    Why
    Root causes cross domains (people arrive, CO2 and power rise together); one model that sees all findings can say so.
    Result
    On 150 held-out incidents (laptop): severity 91.9%, root-cause label 87.8%, valid output 98.7%. On the board, the same prompt run 5 times gives the same severity 97% of the time, 100% with a light guard that never lets the report say "normal" when the rules say warning or worse.
  7. 7 · 27 to 28 Oct · Reasoner latency

    Making the report faster without changing its answers

    What
    Decoding is limited by memory bandwidth: about 60 ms per token, roughly 52 GB/s over 2.6 GB of weights. We measured every lever on the board.
    Kept
    • Polling for NPU completions: 27.8 → 25.8 s per report, identical agent outputs.
    • Two-stage report: a rules-plus-agents report, marked preliminary, appears 1 to 7 s after the event; the reasoner's report replaces it.
    • Split decoding: the headline (title, severity, label) is written once, then 3 groups of fields are written in parallel. 25.7 → 23.7 s on 30 held-out incidents, no loss against the gold answers.
    • Early release: the reasoner leaves the NPU after 3 s idle instead of 20 s, so the next incident's agents don't wait.
    Dropped
    • Speculative decoding (MTP and n-gram): 1% to 20% slower, since small verify batches cost more than one token on this NPU.
    • Hexagon SDK route (upstream llama.cpp and our own 32x4 HVX kernel): reasoner +3% to +6%, agents −4% to −5%; net about zero.
    • Smaller output layer: 4-bit lm_head −4% but changed a label; the pruned lm_head ran once, then the board rebooted at 90 to 94 °C. Held back.
    • Reasoner on the GPU: measured on 29 Oct (Adreno 750, upstream llama.cpp OpenCL, GPU alone): 7.35 / 8.44 / 8.35 tokens/s, about 2.5x slower than the NPU split. Not a latency path.
    • More split groups: split 4 +1%, split 6 does not fit the NPU session, split 3r (fork after the label) 22.2 vs 22.2 s. Split 3 stays.
    Result
    Event to full report through the app: 30 to 43 s → 28 to 39 s, with the preliminary report on screen within seconds.
  8. 8 · 27 to 28 Oct · Agents B50

    B50 becomes the agents' model; a sounder acceptance test

    What
    B50 is mixA50 with half of a further LoRA top-up blended in. We also replaced our acceptance test: a permutation test showed the old one rejected two identical models 71% to 82% of the time.
    Result
    Against mixA50 under the new test: overall +1, severity-weighted +61, story suite +4 (wins minus losses). Through the app: 6 of 6 scenarios done and valid. The next candidate (T30) lost head to head against B50, so B50 stays: accuracy first.
  9. 9 · 28 Oct · Real-world latency

    The real delay is sensor time, not NPU time

    What we found
    Real sensors give one reading per 5 minutes. The AI waits for 8 abnormal readings plus a short batch window, so a real event reached the AI 40 to 55 min after it started, far more than the 30 to 40 s of NPU work.
    What we built
    • Critical fast path: 2 readings in a row past a sensor's critical limit open the incident at once with the rules-only report. The agents and reasoner still run at 8 readings and update the same incident.
    • Gate study: can the agents read younger events (3 to 7 readings)? 2,640 shortened windows, scored per event shape. It decides whether an early, provisional AI report is switched on for some shapes; that feature is built and off by default.
  10. 10 · 28 Oct night · Consolidation

    One deploy, checked through the app, with automatic rollback

    What
    A single script snapshots the known-good board state, picks the split setting, runs a board accuracy and consistency gate for a second reasoner (rsn_v2), deploys the app with the fast path, then runs 6 scenarios and a critical-breach check through the app. Any failure restores the snapshot.
    Result
    See Today for what passed and what runs now.
  11. 11 · 29 Oct morning · Last fixes

    Small, measured fixes; two bigger ideas rejected on evidence

    Kept
    • Contract repairs after the reasoner (normal + needs_more_data gets label unknown; a title location must come from the input): label 83.2% → 87.6% on 386 board answers, 0 new errors.
    • Checklist from the preliminary report: operators can acknowledge and tick while the AI writes; the AI report merges in without losing ticked steps.
    • Campus Assistant: the conversational interface went live: a chat app and an Ask AI button on the same backend, rsn_v1 in its trained chat mode with 9 read-only tools; 9 to 14 s per answer on the board, and it refuses actions.
    • Retrieval seed library: 254 reference reports written by rsn_v1 on the training split, so the preliminary report has similar past incidents from day one.
    Rejected
    • rsn_v2: on the same 106 held-out incidents, severity 87.6% against rsn_v1's 94.7%. rsn_v1 stays.
    • Reasoner on the GPU: about 2.5x slower than the NPU. Split 3r: 22.2 vs 22.2 s.
    Result
    Every build was checked through the app before it stayed live (fast path 5.5 to 5.6 s, AI report on the same incident 42 to 47 s). See Today for the live build.
